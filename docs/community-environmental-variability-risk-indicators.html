Chapter 14 Community Environmental Variability Risk Indicators

14.1 Product Overview

The Community Environmental Vulnerability Risk Indicators (CEVRI) is a national framework that assesses how environmental changes impact marine life and distribute risks across fishing communities by connecting species vulnerability, as calculated by each region’s Climate Vulnerability Assessment (CVA)*, with revenue importance. It evaluates these risks using the specific indicators listed below at the community and regional levels.

The NOAA CVAs score species based on their exposure to projected changes in the environment and their sensitivity or adaptability to handle those changes based on their life history characteristics (such as reproductive rates and diet). CEVRI scores reflect the relationship between the species’ vulnerability assessments and their importance for a community’s revenue.

  • Unit: Community (by species)
  • Summary Group(s): Regional
  • Frequency: Updated Annually
  • Time Series: Annual (2000-2024) the time series goes to the most recent year for which the code is run and updated regularly

14.4 Additional Methods/Decision Rules

14.4.1 Subregion Characterization

Subregions were used to align data to management units where appropriate. Regions are split as follows: Northeast: New England and Mid-Atlantic Southeast*: South Atlantic, Gulf of America, and Florida Keys U.S. Caribbean: Puerto Rico, St. Thomas/St. John, and St. Croix Pacific Islands: Hawai’i, American Samoa (no data), Guam (no data), Northern Mariana Islands (no data), other U.S. Pacific Islands (no data)

*Florida communities not located in the Keys were split between the South Atlantic and Gulf subregions by drawing an imaginary line splitting the state equally in two and attributing communities to either side.

14.4.2 Community Characterization

Communities are operationalized differently for different regions and subregions depending on data availability and structure: Northeast: Communities represent the ports of landing as reported by dealers. Southeast: Communities are defined by linking reported landings to dealer addresses by dealer identification number. Hawai’i: Landings are associated with the Census county division for which they were reported. Puerto Rico: Landings are attributed to the municipality for which they were reported. USVI: Landings are reported by submunicipality but they were aggregated by island/island group.

14.4.3 Treatment of Landings and Species data

14.4.3.1 Puget Sound Rockfish Species

Species of rockfish endemic to Puget Sound were scored by the CVA effort differently from those occurring elsewhere in the region. Therefore, for any communities adjacent to the Sound, the calculations use the Puget Sound scores for all rockfish species classified.

14.4.3.2 AK vessels landing in the West Coast

Historically, vessels from Alaska, fishing in AK waters, land a significant portion of their catch in the West Coast, particularly Seattle. CEVRI calculations for the West Coast include landings from these AK vessels and apply West Coast species CVA scores due to lack of AK specific CVAs to date.

14.4.3.3 Puerto Rico Landings Correction Factor

Landings for Puerto Rico used the corrected values that are estimated by the SEFSC due to perceived underreporting. The correction factor is calculated by coast, i.e. the North, East, South and West where port samplers intercept some of the trips throughout Puerto Rico and estimate the amount of underreporting. Additionally, for the last 5 years due to a variety of reasons (e.g., Covid), 2020-2024 landings use the average correction factors by coast for 2014-2019. The CEVRI calculations use the corrected values to maintain consistency with other documentation and analyses conducted in the region.
